Is using the internet a fundamental right? The Supreme Court says yes, and children have it too 🌐⚖️

The Supreme Court has held that the right to access and use the internet is a fundamental right under Article 21 (right to life) read with Article 19(1)(a) (freedom of speech, which includes receiving and sharing information). The court noted that for many children, especially in remote areas, the internet is often their only source of learning.

The court refused to back a blanket social media ban for children. It said keeping kids off the internet treats the victim as the problem, may push them to less safe platforms, and age checks could mean constant collection of sensitive data. But safeguards are still allowed: parental consent and oversight, a "child mode", private accounts by default and limits on contact from strangers. The government can still block content that is harmful in itself, like adult websites.

The court also set out stronger steps against online child sexual abuse material, including a national database so that content removed by one platform is flagged on others, easy and anonymous reporting, and acknowledgement of complaints within 24 hours. The Centre has to report its progress on 10 December 2026.

Case: Just Rights for Children Alliance v. S. Harish, 2026 INSC 1149, 2026 LiveLaw (SC) 1063
Bench: Justices J.B. Pardiwala and K. Vinod Chandran, Supreme Court of India
