Got a video call saying you are under "digital arrest"? It is a scam. Hang up 📵⚖️

Myth 1: "Police can arrest me on a call." No. The government's Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) has made it clear that CBI, police, customs, ED or judges do not arrest anyone on a video call. On 29 October 2026, the Chief Justice of India asked who, in a democracy, believes they can be arrested on a mobile phone.

Myth 2: "If I lose money, the bank owes me nothing." Not necessarily. The Supreme Court has said the RBI's new rules must make banks compensate victims where weak safeguards or the bank's lack of due diligence helped the fraud, and the compensation should not be less than the amount lost. The court also wants banks to pause unusual transactions, alert the police and verify them first. These rules are still being framed: RBI is to notify them by 30 November and bring them into force by 31 December 2026.

Myth 3: "Reporting won't help." Report at once on the national cybercrime helpline 1930 or at cybercrime.gov.in, and go to your local police station. Remember, scammers may show fake court orders; the case that started this matter involved forged judicial orders.

Case: Suo motu proceedings on digital arrest scams, Supreme Court of India (hearing of 29 Oct 2026)
