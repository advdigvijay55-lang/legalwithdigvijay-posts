Can elderly in-laws ask their daughter-in-law to leave their home? The Delhi High Court says yes, but not onto the street 🏠⚖️

A woman had lived in her in-laws' Delhi house since her marriage in 2008, along with her son, who is now in Class XII. Her in-laws, aged about 70 and 64, alleged harassment and moved the authorities under the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007. The District Magistrate refused eviction, but the Divisional Commissioner allowed it, giving her roughly three weeks to move once alternate housing was arranged.

Justice Swarana Kanta Sharma said a daughter-in-law's right to live in the shared household under the Domestic Violence Act is not absolute, and must be weighed against senior citizens' right to live in peace and dignity. The eviction was correct in principle, but the order was too vague to protect her right to a home.

So the Court set clear terms: she chooses a two-bedroom home within four weeks, the in-laws pay rent up to Rs 35,000 a month plus deposit, brokerage and shifting costs, with six months' rent in advance, and she moves two weeks after that. The rent continues until her right of residence is finally decided in the DV or matrimonial case.

Case: Namrata Yadav v. Ramesh Kumar Yadav & Ors., W.P.(C) 12654/2026, 2026 LiveLaw (Del) 1113
Court: Delhi High Court, Justice Swarana Kanta Sharma (19 November 2026)
