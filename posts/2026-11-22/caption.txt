Bought a flat and assumed the parking space came with it? Not unless your sale deed says so, says the Calcutta High Court 🚗🏢

In 1992, the earlier owners of a West Bengal Housing Board flat applied for a parking space and deposited Rs 7,000. In March 1993 they sold the flat by a registered deed that said nothing about parking, but in a separate letter promised to hand over the space once the Board allotted it. The Board decided in 1997 to allot space BG-9 to the earlier owner, but never executed a conveyance, and in 2019 it revoked that decision.

The buyer argued that under Section 8 of the Transfer of Property Act he had stepped into the sellers' shoes. Justice Partha Sarathi Sen disagreed. The buyer could not show that the parking was an inseparable part of the flat or that it was included in the deed, and the Board had never conveyed the space to the sellers in the first place. If anyone was aggrieved, it was the sellers, not the buyer. The writ petition was dismissed.

The lesson for home buyers: a side promise from the seller is not enough. If parking matters to you, make sure the registered deed itself clearly includes it.

Case: Shrawan Kumar Agarwal v. The West Bengal Housing Board & Ors., WPA 2129 of 2020
Court: Calcutta High Court, Justice Partha Sarathi Sen (19 November 2026)
