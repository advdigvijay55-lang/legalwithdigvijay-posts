A maintenance case in the Family Court does not pull your domestic violence case away from the Magistrate 🏛️⚖️

The Punjab and Haryana High Court has refused a husband's request to move his wife's domestic violence case from a Judicial Magistrate in Gurugram to the Family Court, Gurugram. He argued that a maintenance case between the couple was already pending in the Family Court, and that running two cases on overlapping facts would cause confusion and conflicting findings.

The Court explained that under Section 27 of the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005, the Magistrate is the competent court for a Section 12 application. Section 26 only allows a woman to seek DV reliefs inside a case already pending in a civil, family or criminal court. That limited power does not let the Family Court take over a separate DV case. The Court also said that jurisdiction comes only from the law, not from convenience, cost or the consent of the parties.

The Court relied on the Supreme Court's reading of Sections 26 and 27 in Shaurabh Kumar Tripathi v. Vidhi Rawal, and on rulings of the Kerala and Delhi High Courts. It noted that the Bombay High Court had taken a different view. The transfer plea was dismissed, and the parties were told to appear before the Magistrate.
