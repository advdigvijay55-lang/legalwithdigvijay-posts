Asking for more dowry after the wedding is still a crime ⚖️💍

The Madhya Pradesh High Court has said that a dowry case does not need proof of any deal on the amount made before the marriage. Under Section 2 of the Dowry Prohibition Act, dowry includes property given or agreed to be given before, at, or any time after the marriage, if it is connected with the marriage. So a demand raised after the wedding is covered, and the demand itself is the offence under Section 4.

In this case, the bride's family had given ₹17 lakh, jewellery and household items at the 2007 wedding. The husband's side allegedly said they had expected ₹25 lakh and harassed her for the remaining ₹8 lakh. The trial court convicted them, but the Sessions Court acquitted them. The High Court set aside the acquittal and restored the husband's conviction under Section 498A IPC and Section 4 of the Dowry Act, with one year of rigorous imprisonment and a fine. The appeal against the father-in-law ended because he had died.

The court also said a dowry demand can be proved by oral evidence, minor differences in witnesses' accounts do not destroy the core case, and a wife need not file a police complaint for every incident. Each case still depends on its own evidence.
