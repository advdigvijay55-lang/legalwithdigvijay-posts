Police held you on Monday but 'arrested' you on Tuesday? The Supreme Court says the 24-hour clock started on Monday ⏱️⚖️

In a drug case, the accused came under the search team's control around 2 PM on 29 September 2025. The arrest was recorded only the next day, after an unexplained 18-hour gap, and the accused was produced before a magistrate at 4:45 PM on 30 September. The agency argued the 24 hours began only from the recorded arrest.

The Court disagreed. Under Article 22(2), the 24 hours run from actual detention, not from the arrest memo. Police cannot create a 'buffer zone' of custody before a formal arrest, and only necessary travel time to the court can be excluded. Even within 24 hours, detention must be reasonable under Section 58 BNSS. Magistrates must record the actual time of detention and can refuse remand if the hold was unreasonably long.

The Court also said strict NDPS bail conditions under Section 37 cannot override these constitutional safeguards, and ordered the accused released. Remember: this was about the breach of the 24-hour rule, not a finding of innocence.

Case: Sujit Das v. Union of India (Supreme Court, 5 Nov 2026)
Bench: Justice Ujjal Bhuyan and Justice Atul S Chandurkar
