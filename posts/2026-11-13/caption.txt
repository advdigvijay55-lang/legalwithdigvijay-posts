Missed the running test by just 3 seconds - and the High Court said she must still be appointed ♿⚖️

The Jharkhand High Court has held that a candidate with a disability is entitled to reasonable accommodation in a government recruitment test. A woman with a locomotor disability had applied for a Chowkidar post reserved for persons with disabilities. She cleared the written exam, but in the physical test she had to run with other candidates with no extra time or support, and finished in 10 minutes 3 seconds against a 10-minute limit. Her name was then dropped from the merit list.

The Court relied on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016. Section 20 bans discrimination in government employment and requires reasonable accommodation, and Section 2(y) defines it as suitable changes and support so a disabled person can compete fairly. The Court called this a positive duty of the State, not a favour, and said no rule or notification can go beyond the Act made by Parliament.

Since she had cleared the written test, missed by only 3 seconds, and reserved disabled posts were still vacant, the Court held she was entitled to appointment. The takeaway: if you have a disability, ask for the accommodation the law gives you - a missing rule is not a valid reason to deny it.

Case: Juthika Pal v. State of Jharkhand
Court: Jharkhand High Court, Justice Deepak Roshan, reported 11 November 2026
