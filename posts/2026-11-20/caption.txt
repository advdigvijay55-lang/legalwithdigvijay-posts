Is the birth date on your Aadhaar card proof of your age? Not by itself, says the Delhi High Court 🪪⚖️

Rosamma Joy says she worked for a firm from December 1981 and was removed on 20 May 2017, soon after she asked for revised minimum wages. The employer said she had turned 58 and retired that day, relying only on her Aadhaar card, which showed a birth date of 20 May 1959. But in 2010, when she was registered with ESIC, the employer itself had given her birth date as 20 May 1960.

Justice Manoj Kumar Ohri held that Aadhaar is mainly proof of identity and is not, by itself, proof of date of birth. The Court relied on a UIDAI office memorandum of 20 December 2018, which says birth dates in Aadhaar are recorded from documents or self-declaration, and followed the Supreme Court's ruling in Saroj v. IFFCO-Tokio General Insurance. Since the employer claimed she had retired, it had to prove it, and it produced nothing except the Aadhaar card.

The ESIC record was made years before any dispute, so the employer could not switch to a different date later. The Labour Court's finding that her removal was illegal, with full back wages till 20 May 2018, was upheld and the employer's petition was dismissed. Aadhaar remains valid proof of identity; only its birth date is not conclusive proof of age on its own.

Case: M/s J. J. Electronics v. Smt. Rosamma Joy, W.P.(C) 16476/2022
Court: Delhi High Court, Justice Manoj Kumar Ohri (reported 19 November 2026)
