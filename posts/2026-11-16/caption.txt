Being richer does not give grandparents a better claim to a child than his mother 👩‍👦⚖️

The Allahabad High Court has dismissed an appeal by a boy's paternal grandparents who wanted to keep him away from his mother. After her husband's death in November 2025, the mother says the grandparents kept her 8-year-old son with them, while her 4-year-old daughter stayed with her. When the police did not help, she filed a habeas corpus petition, and on 27 May 2026 a Single Judge handed custody of the boy to her.

In appeal, the grandparents argued that they had more money, that the mother earned only about Rs 20,000 a month from tuition, and that she got custody just because she is the mother. The Division Bench disagreed. It said better financial resources, on their own, cannot be a reason to take a minor son away from his mother. The Single Judge had looked at the child's tender age, continuity of care, emotional bond, schooling and Section 6 of the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956, and the Bench found it a well-structured order.

The Court suggested that the grandparents could support the children financially instead of seeking custody. On visitation, it left them free to approach the district court or start fresh proceedings. Remember, custody is always decided on the child's welfare in each case.

Case: Manoj Sahlot & Another v. State of U.P. & 6 Others, 2026 LiveLaw (AB) 941
Court: Allahabad High Court, Chief Justice Arun Bhansali and Justice Kshitij Shailendra, reported 15 November 2026
