Posted something online and now there's an FIR? Here's what the law actually says 📱⚖️

Myth 1: "An FIR means instant arrest." No. The Karnataka High Court has said that registering a case does not by itself justify an arrest. Police must follow Section 35 of the BNSS and the Supreme Court's guidelines in Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar.

Myth 2: "Sharing a news report is always a crime." No. In this case, an X page admin was booked under Section 353(2) BNS for reposting a news report about a stalking incident. The court said this offence needs an intention to create enmity between groups. The post named no group, so the FIR was quashed.

Myth 3: "Every new complaint means a new FIR." No. Police had filed seven FIRs over one incident, which the court called shocking. Police must first check whether a case on the same post or incident is already registered. The court asked Karnataka's DG and IGP to make sure every police officer follows these guidelines.

Keep in mind: this is not a free pass. Posts meant to spread hatred between groups can still be a crime. Check facts before you share.

Case: Sri Sandeep Kumar v. State of Karnataka, Crl. P. No. 13010 of 2026, 2026 LiveLaw (Kar) 425
Bench: Justice M. Nagaprasanna, Karnataka High Court
