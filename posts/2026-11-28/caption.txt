Your car is insured. But is the friend in the passenger seat? 🚗⚠️

Myth: "My vehicle is insured, so anyone riding in it is covered." The Supreme Court has said this is not automatically true. A basic "Act Only" (third-party) policy under Section 147 of the Motor Vehicles Act does not, by itself, cover a person riding free in a private vehicle. The law covers passengers of buses and taxis separately, which shows not every occupant is included. A comprehensive or package policy may cover occupants, depending on its terms.

The case: in 2012, a jeep driven by its owner overturned and a passenger died. The Tribunal at Latur awarded his wife and two sons Rs 7,79,800 with interest and asked the insurer to pay first and recover from the owner. The Bombay High Court removed that direction. The Supreme Court agreed the insurer was not liable under the policy, and said only it can use Article 142 to order such payment. Treating the case as exceptional, it asked the insurer to pay the unpaid award and recover it from the owner.

What to do: check what your motor policy actually covers, ask your insurer about cover for occupants, and remember that without it, the owner may have to pay.

Case: Sukumarbai & Ors. v. United India Insurance Company Ltd. & Anr., 2026 INSC 1272
Bench: Justices Vikram Nath, Sandeep Mehta and Aparesh Kumar Singh, Supreme Court (25 Nov 2026)
