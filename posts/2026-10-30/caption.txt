Can the law keep women out of a job "for their own safety"? The Kerala High Court says no 👩‍💼⚖️

A widow whose husband worked at a toddy shop in Thrissur asked for a job there on compassionate grounds after his death. She was turned down because Rule 7(37) of the Kerala Abkari Shops Disposal Rules, 2002 barred women from working in toddy shops in any role. The State defended the rule as a safety measure, saying toddy shops are often in remote places.

Justice P. Gopinath struck the rule down. The Court found it was neither justified nor proportionate, and that it violates Articles 14, 15, 19 and 21 of the Constitution. Relying on the Supreme Court's ruling in Anuj Garg v. Hotel Association of India, the Court said the State's job is to make workplaces safe for women, not to bar them from the work they choose.

What happens now? Her claim for compassionate appointment must be considered without applying the rule, and any licence condition based on it cannot stand. The Court did not itself order that she be given the job.

Case: Sophy v. State of Kerala, WP(C) No. 27762 of 2024, 2026 LiveLaw (Ker) 606 (Kerala High Court)
