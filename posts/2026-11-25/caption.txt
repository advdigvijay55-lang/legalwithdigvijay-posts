Staying in a PG? The Delhi High Court says you are a licensee, not a tenant 🏠⚖️

A woman ran a PG for girl students and working women in Shakarpur, Delhi. A resident given one bed in a shared room in 2021 allegedly stopped paying, quarrelled with others and refused to leave after the arrangement was ended. She said she was a tenant and had paid Rs 2 lakh to buy two rooms under a plain-paper agreement.

Justice Neena Bansal Krishna explained that a lease gives a person an interest in the property, while a licence only gives permission to use it. In a PG, the operator keeps overall control, so the arrangement is closer to a licence. The Delhi Rent Control Act does not protect it, and once the licence is ended, the resident must vacate.

The Court also said an unregistered plain-paper agreement to buy does not give a right to keep living there, though she can pursue a separate case on it. The possession order was upheld and her appeal dismissed. Remember, courts look at the real nature of each arrangement, so not every rented room is a licence.

Case: Payal v. Pooja Gupta, RSA 166/2026, 2026:DHC:10313
Court: Delhi High Court, Justice Neena Bansal Krishna (20 November 2026)
