Posted your photo on Instagram? That does not give anyone the right to misuse it 📸⚖️

Myth: "If my photo is public, anyone can use it." The Karnataka High Court has said that a publicly available photo does not, by itself, allow another person to misuse it. A photo posted for ordinary and legitimate reasons cannot be treated as consent to use it in obscene material, or to pass such material around.

The case: a woman complained that her Instagram ID and photos were used with an obscene video shared in WhatsApp groups. Her ID and contact details spread further, and she started getting unwanted messages. Police filed a charge sheet against five people under Sections 66C (identity theft) and 67 (publishing obscene material electronically) of the IT Act.

The accused said the woman in the photos and the woman in the video were different people. The court noted that a forensic report found similar facial features, said this question must be decided at trial, and held that it cannot hold a mini-trial while deciding a quashing plea. The petition was dismissed. Remember, this does not mean anyone is guilty yet; the trial court will decide the case on the evidence.

Case: Dheeraj G Kunder & Others v. State of Karnataka, Crl. P. No. 16549 of 2025, 2026:KHC:57298
Bench: Justice Suraj Govindaraj, Karnataka High Court (order dated 12 Oct 2026)
