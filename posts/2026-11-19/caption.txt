No bank account? You can still send or receive money through a bank 💸🏦

On 11 November 2026, the Reserve Bank of India issued the RBI (Domestic Money Transfer) Directions, 2026, which apply immediately. They bring the rules for small cash-based money transfers into one place, replacing two older circulars from 2011 and 2024. The limits stay the same: a walk-in customer can pay cash at a bank branch and send up to Rs 50,000 by NEFT to a bank account. Through a Business Correspondent or ATM, the limit is Rs 5,000 per transaction and Rs 25,000 a month per sender. Money can also be paid out in cash to someone with no bank account, up to Rs 10,000 per transaction and Rs 25,000 a month per receiver.

To use the facility, the sender is registered with a verified mobile number and a self-certified Officially Valid Document under RBI's KYC rules, and every transaction needs an additional factor of authentication. The sender's details go with the NEFT or IMPS message, which is tagged as a cash-based transfer. For cash pay-outs, the bank records the receiver's name and address.

Banks must run velocity checks, alert customers when money is credited, investigate sudden spikes in credits to an account or group of accounts, and report suspicious transactions. Transfers should be real-time or near real-time. Complaints fall under the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026. Card-to-card transfers are not covered by these rules.

Source: RBI (Domestic Money Transfer) Directions, 2026, RBI/DPSS/2026-27/476, dated 11 November 2026, issued under Section 18 read with Section 10(2) of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007
