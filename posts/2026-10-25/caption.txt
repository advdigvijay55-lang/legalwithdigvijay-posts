Working on contract does not take away your right to maternity leave 🤰⚖️

The Rajasthan High Court has said that the type of job a woman holds, whether regular, casual or contractual, cannot by itself be a reason to deny her maternity benefits when the legal conditions are met. A clause in a contract cannot override the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961, because Section 27 of the Act gives it overriding effect over any contrary agreement.

In this case, a woman was hired as a consultant by the Rajasthan State Pollution Control Board on a one-year contract in August 2022. She asked for six months of maternity leave from 26 December 2022. It was refused only because her appointment order allowed government holidays and no other leave. The court said the refusal never examined whether she was actually eligible, and her whole leave period fell within her contract term.

The High Court quashed the rejection and directed that she be given 180 days of maternity benefits, with the remuneration due under the law, within three months of the employer receiving a certified copy of the judgment. Each case still depends on its own facts and the conditions under the law.

Case: Smt. Shivangi Khandelwal v. State of Rajasthan & Anr., S.B. Civil Writ Petition No. 1561/2023 | 2026:RJ-JD:49861 | 2026 LiveLaw (Raj) 449 | Justice Bipin Gupta, Rajasthan High Court (Jodhpur)
