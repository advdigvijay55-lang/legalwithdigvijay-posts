Ever booked a flight and found extra charges at the last step? New rules aim to fix that ✈️⚖️

The Ministry of Civil Aviation has notified the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026. These rules replace the Aircraft Rules, 1937 and come into force on 1 January 2027. Along with safety, licensing and airports, they also deal with how airlines show their fares.

Under Rule 192, airlines must publish fares on their websites and apps, show the total amount payable while booking and on the ticket, and give a separate break-up of base fare, taxes, fees, cancellation charges and refund conditions. Airlines must file tariff details with the DGCA, which will monitor fares. In an extraordinary situation, the Centre can issue written directions on fares in the public interest, but they must be proportionate and only for a specified period.

Please note: there is no cap on ticket prices. Airlines still set their own fares, as they have since 1994. Reports also point out that the old rule's specific mention of excessive or predatory pricing does not appear in the new Rule 192. The new rules do not apply to this festive season.

Law: Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026 (Rule 192), Ministry of Civil Aviation, in force from 1 Jan 2027
