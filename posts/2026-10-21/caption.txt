A disability certificate does not tell the whole story, says the Supreme Court 🚑⚖️

A 43-year-old man suffered severe head and brain injuries in a scooter accident in 2016. He now needs an attendant for the rest of his life. The medical board rated his disability at 75.5%, and the High Court calculated compensation on that basis.

The Supreme Court explained the difference between medical disability (the injury doctors certify) and functional disability (how much the injury affects a person's daily life and work). Functional disability can be higher than the medical figure. Since this man cannot do his own work, let alone earn, the court treated his loss of earning capacity as 100%. Courts must give realistic compensation based on what the victim could have earned.

The court raised his total award to about ₹77.84 lakh, against ₹36.22 lakh given by the High Court, and increased attendant charges from ₹1 lakh to ₹25.20 lakh. The injured scooter driver was assessed at 80% functional disability against a medical 62%. The extra amounts carry 6.5% yearly interest and the insurer must deposit them within six weeks. This does not mean every injury is 100%; each case depends on its facts.

Case: Sunil Kumar (Totally Disabled, Represented by Wife) v. IFFCO Tokio General Insurance Company Limited, 2026 INSC 1145
Bench: Justices S.V.N. Bhatti and N.V. Anjaria, Supreme Court of India
