Cancer treatment in India is set to become cheaper from 9 November 2026 💊⚖️

The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) has capped trade margins at 30% of the MRP for 137 non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs. The cap covers every combination, strength and dosage form of these drugs, including patented medicines unless they are exempted. NPPA used its special power under Paragraph 19 of the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013, on the advice of an expert committee.

What does this mean for your bill? The new MRP is based on what the company charged stockists in March 2026. For example, a drug sold to stockists at ₹70 with an MRP of ₹300 must now cost no more than ₹100 plus GST. Prices are expected to fall by up to 70%, but the actual cut depends on each medicine. If the current MRP is already lower, it stays. In 2019, a similar cap on 42 cancer drugs cut prices by up to 90% for 526 brands.

From 9 November, chemists and hospitals must charge the reduced price and display revised prices. You can check prices on the Pharma Sahi Daam app or the NPPA website, and overcharging can be reported to NPPA. Companies also cannot cut production without NPPA's permission.

Source: NPPA order S.O. 5690(E) dated 17 October 2026, under Paragraph 19, Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013
