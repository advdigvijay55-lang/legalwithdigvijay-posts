Acquitted in 2018, but still in jail until 2025 ⚖️⛓️

The Chhattisgarh High Court acquitted Devilal Gond of a murder charge on 20 January 2018, and the State did not appeal. But the news of his acquittal never reached the prison. He walked out only on 19 May 2025, after a records check found the lapse. By then he had spent 7 years, 3 months and 21 days in jail with no legal basis, from about age 28 to about age 35.

The High Court awarded him Rs 2 lakh. On 16 November 2026, the Supreme Court raised this to Rs 30 lakh, payable by the State within four weeks. The Court called the detention an egregious violation of the right to life and liberty under Article 21. The State blamed a communication gap between the jail and legal aid officials, but the Court said this showed poor coordination and institutional failure by all the authorities. Compensation, it said, must help a person rebuild his life and cannot be a token amount.

The Court also noted that India has no law setting out compensation for wrongful imprisonment, and referred to the Law Commission's 277th Report. All High Courts must now consider safeguards like those in Madhya Pradesh, including prompt communication of acquittal and release orders and an online system linking courts, police and prisons.

Case: Devilal Gond v. State of Chhattisgarh & Ors., 2026 INSC 1226, 2026 LiveLaw (SC) 1153
Court: Supreme Court of India, Justices Augustine George Masih and Sanjeev Sachdeva, 16 November 2026
