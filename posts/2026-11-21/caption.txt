Missed a life insurance premium? Your family's cover may be gone 📄⚠️

Myth: "One missed premium doesn't matter, the insurer will still pay." The Gauhati High Court has made it clear that this is wrong. If a premium is not paid within the grace period, the policy lapses under its own terms, and a death claim made after that can be rejected. A bounced auto-debit is not payment and does not extend the grace period.

The case: a man had paid 28 monthly premiums on an LIC policy through bank auto-debit. His 29th premium, due on 18 November 2020, bounced because of low balance, and nothing was paid in the 15-day grace period. He died in a road accident on 16 December 2020. LIC rejected his wife's claim. The Insurance Ombudsman ordered LIC to pay Rs 4 lakh, but the High Court set that award aside, saying nothing is payable under a lapsed policy. It relied on the Supreme Court's ruling in LIC v. Mani Ram (2005).

What to do: know your policy's grace period (in this policy, 15 days for monthly premiums and at least 30 days for other modes), keep enough balance on the due date, and check that every premium has actually been paid.

Case: Life Insurance Corporation of India v. The Insurance Ombudsman & 2 Ors., WP(C) No. 135 of 2023, 2026:GAU-AS:16917
Bench: Justice Devashis Baruah, Gauhati High Court (12 Nov 2026)
