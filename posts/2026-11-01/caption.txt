Borrowed from a private moneylender in Maharashtra and now facing a cheque bounce case? This Supreme Court ruling matters 💸⚖️

In May 2026, the Supreme Court revived cheque bounce cases over promissory-note loans given under the Tamil Nadu moneylenders law. An applicant facing such proceedings then asked the court to make clear that this ruling does not help unlicensed moneylenders in Maharashtra.

On 29 October 2026, the court explained that Maharashtra law is different. Under the Maharashtra Money-Lending (Regulation) Act, 2014, lending on a promissory note without a licence is prohibited (Section 4) and such a loan cannot be enforced (Section 13). So it is not a legally enforceable debt for a cheque bounce case under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, and the moneylender must show a licence to the court at the very start.

But no case is closed automatically. The court said its May ruling did not decide either state's moneylending law, so no formal clarification was needed. Whether the Section 13 bar applies in a particular case is for the trial court or a higher court to decide. If you face such a case, raise the point there, and never ignore a court summons.

Case: In re Vyomesh Mahipatray Shah, in M/s Mansi Finance (Chennai) Ltd. v. M. Lalitha & Ors., MA No. 3002 of 2026 in Criminal Appeal No. 2849 of 2026, 2026 INSC 1174 (29 Oct 2026)
Bench: Justice Prashant Kumar Mishra and Justice N.V. Anjaria
