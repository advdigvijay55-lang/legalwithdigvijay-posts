Government employee arrested and kept in jail for more than 48 hours? Here is what the Supreme Court says ⚖️🏛️

The Supreme Court has said that once a public servant is arrested and remains in judicial custody for more than 48 hours, he is under "deemed suspension". No separate order is needed for it. The case involved a hospital manager in Samastipur, Bihar, accused under the Prevention of Corruption Act of demanding Rs 4,000 to clear a worker's wages. He spent about 15 days in judicial custody before the Sessions Court gave him bail.

Almost a year later, the Patna High Court cancelled his bail. The Supreme Court set that order aside and restored bail. It said that in cases of this type, bail should ordinarily be granted, keeping in mind the maximum punishment possible, unless the accused has criminal antecedents or the facts are very gross. The Court also agreed that the size of the bribe is not a relevant factor for deciding bail.

The Court was disturbed that the State had revoked his suspension and posted him back to the same hospital and post, and asked the State to look into it. Keep in mind: bail is not acquittal. The order does not decide whether the bribe was taken; the trial will.

Case: Vishwajeet Singh @ Bishwajeet Ramanand v. State of Bihar, Criminal Appeal No. 5282/2026, 2026 LiveLaw (SC) 1146
Bench: Justices JB Pardiwala and Manmohan, Supreme Court, 6 Nov 2026
